\begin{abstract}
Over-the-air fuzzing is still effective at finding vulnerabilities in the
wireless stacks of smart-home devices, but turning a noisy crash log into a
replayable proof of concept remains slow, because the unpredictable medium makes
the test \emph{oracle} a reproduction tool relies on answer inconsistently, which
the precise input-minimisation algorithms cannot tolerate. We introduce
\emph{Robust Delta-Debugging} (RDD), which approximates the stable verdict those
algorithms assume: a truncated Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) folds the
noisy outcomes of repeated attempts into one decision with a bounded
false-positive rate; a two-tier crash-identity oracle uses a deterministic matcher
and escalates only its uncertain cases to a single open-weight language model; a
suppressor-robust minimiser drives delta debugging and credits a recipe only
after an independent re-confirmation under the same bound. On six real Zephyr
Bluetooth-controller bugs under a simulated noisy channel, a prototype recovers
0.848 of reproductions with no false credit in 336 campaigns, against 0.759 and
0.137 for a re-implementation of the state-of-the-art tool's strategy, at five
times the device reads; on a 75,000-campaign synthetic sweep it recovers 0.824
against 0.266. The advantage is a property of the simulator's noise assumptions:
with report variation and phantom crashes switched off the baseline recovers
0.978 with no false credit at a fifth of the cost. We state these dependencies,
and the experiments that would test them on real radios, as the research agenda.
\end{abstract}
