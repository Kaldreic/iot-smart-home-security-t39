\begin{abstract}
Over-the-air fuzzing still finds vulnerabilities in the wireless stacks of
smart-home devices, but turning a crash log into a replayable proof of concept
remains slow: the unpredictable medium makes the reproduction \emph{oracle}
answer inconsistently, which precise minimisation algorithms cannot tolerate. We introduce \emph{Robust
Delta-Debugging} (RDD), which approximates the stable verdict those algorithms
assume. A truncated Sequential Probability Ratio Test folds repeated noisy
attempts into one decision with a bounded false-positive rate; a two-tier
crash-identity oracle escalates only what a deterministic matcher cannot settle
to a single open-weight language model; a suppressor-robust minimiser drives
delta debugging and credits a recipe only after an independent re-confirmation.
On six real Zephyr Bluetooth-controller bugs under a simulated noisy channel, a
prototype recovers 0.848 of reproductions with no false credit in 336 campaigns,
against 0.759 and 0.137 for a re-implementation of AirBugCatcher's strategy, at
five times the device reads; on a 75,000-campaign synthetic sweep
it recovers 0.824 against 0.266. The advantage is a property of the simulator's
noise assumptions: with report variation and phantom crashes switched off, the
baseline recovers 0.978 with no false credit at a fifth of the cost. The research
agenda is to test these dependencies on real radios.
\end{abstract}
