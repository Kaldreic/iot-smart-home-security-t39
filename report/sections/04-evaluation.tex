\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

We tested the proposed approach using a working prototype, treating its results as
proof-of-concept evidence for the research direction, not as final system results.
The analysis compares RDD to a faithful re-implementation of
\textsc{AirBugCatcher}'s reproduction strategy~\cite{airbugcatcher2024} (bounded
enumeration with exact crash-signature matching) on the same simulated channel, so
any differences are due to the method, not the environment. The main questions are
whether RDD improves results on real bugs and whether it performs well under tough
conditions. A third question, whether it works off the shelf and end to end, is
discussed later. The main metrics are paired: \emph{genuine} recovery is the
fraction of campaigns that return a PoC which provably crashes the correct bug
(checked against a channel-off ground truth the tool never sees), and \emph{false
credit} is the fraction where a PoC does not reproduce the intended fault. The
\emph{size-gap} shows how much larger a recovered recipe is compared to the true
minimum. Table~\ref{tab:headlines} summarises the results.

\subsection{Real bugs, head to head}\label{ssec:eval-real}

The evaluation examines six controller bugs, labelled A--F, in the Zephyr Bluetooth
Low Energy stack compiled for the \texttt{native\_sim} software target. One is a
disclosed vulnerability with a CVE identifier, a division-by-zero in
connection-parameter handling. The other five are reachable controller faults
exercised through harness patches on the same vulnerable Zephyr controller, one of
them sharing the first bug's binary. % multibug.py + zephyr-targets/README: A = CVE-2024-4785; B--F reachable faults; build-multibug builds A+C
Only one of the six is a named CVE, and the report deliberately distinguishes the
formally disclosed vulnerability from the others.

When run through the full pipeline (fuzz campaign, shared crash-grouping, a
backward window per bug, then either minimiser), RDD achieves a genuine recovery
rate of 0.863 with zero false credit, while the baseline gets 0.759 with
0.137 false credit. % B1 headtohead.json
Across the eight seeds RDD spans $[0.76, 0.93]$ and the baseline $[0.67, 0.81]$ (paired per seed).
RDD also produces more concise recipes: their extra size over the true minimum is
about four times smaller (0.12 vs.\ 0.48 extra packets), and most are exactly
minimal. The 0.863 figure is the true end-to-end result, including a
crash-grouping front-end shared by both tools, which sometimes assigns a trace to
the wrong bug and limits what either minimiser can achieve. If the front-end is
removed and reduction starts directly from each bug's window, isolating the
minimiser and its oracle, RDD recovers 0.967 of reproductions over thirty
seeds against the baseline's 0.867. % realbench_robust.json arm_b_plus 0.9667 vs arm_a 0.8667
The trade-off for RDD is the number of reads: about 45 simulator queries per
campaign, compared to 9 for the baseline, mostly used for SPRT confirmation and
some escalations to the open-weight model.

Why focus on the baseline's 0.137 false credit? In a workflow for human
analysts, a false credit is costly because it leads experts to investigate crashes
that were not actually reproduced. RDD eliminates this error. This is most evident
with the real suppressor bug, where a later packet can hide the crash: plain
bisection fails completely (0.000), the baseline gets 0.875 with 0.125 false
credit, and RDD recovers everything with zero false credit. % suppressor: B1 headtohead.json (AirBug 0.875/0.125, RDD 1.0); plain-ddmin 0.000 from B3 levers.json ablation
This scenario is exactly what the robust minimiser is designed for, and the next
experiment tests it further.

\subsection{Resilience at scale}\label{ssec:eval-scale}

Because only a handful of real emulable bugs exist, we probe robustness with a
large synthetic corpus anchored to the real target: fifteen thousand bugs across
six adversarial regimes (standard, high-cardinality, suppressor, far-trigger,
deep-noise, and wide-window), giving seventy-five thousand campaigns per tool. The
regimes deliberately twist the structures that trouble delta debugging: large input
spaces, non-monotone suppressors, awkward trigger positions, and noise deep enough
to push a true reproduce-rate into the SPRT's indifference zone. At this scale the
L3 tier uses a deterministic site-string rule rather than the open-weight model, a
conservative stand-in that keeps the sweep model-free and reproducible; the model
itself is exercised in the head-to-head and live runs. RDD averages
0.916 genuine recovery at zero false credit. The baseline averages 0.282 at
0.638 (Fig.~\ref{fig:regimes}). % B2 resilience.json OVERALL
The $75{,}000$-campaign bootstrap intervals, resampled by bug, are RDD
$[0.914, 0.918]$ and baseline $[0.276, 0.288]$, far from overlapping.
RDD pays for the gap in reads, as on the real bugs, roughly 149 device queries
per campaign against the baseline's 37.

Looking at each regime gives a clearer picture. The baseline does not fail equally
in all cases: it performs best in the standard and suppressor regimes (0.378 and
0.371) but fails almost completely on high-cardinality inputs (0.053), where
the true recipe is larger than its fixed size limit and repetition cannot help.
RDD stays above 0.94 in all regimes except deep-noise, because its minimiser is
not limited by size and its oracle samples through the channel instead of just
averaging. Deep-noise (0.750) is the only exception, and this is due to the
SPRT's indifference band~\cite{sprt1945}, not a flaw in the implementation. RDD's recipes here are also larger ($1.30$ vs
$0.70$ extra packets), as it reproduces harder cases the baseline misses. The
band is a design choice: making $p_0$ tighter or increasing the budget reduces the
band, trading more reads for better recall. RDD accepts lower recall in deep-noise
to keep false credit at zero, which is the purpose of the soundness gate.

\subsection{Where the advantage comes from}\label{ssec:eval-levers}

RDD consists of multiple components, prompting the question of which are most
impactful. To address this, we evaluated each part individually on real bugs,
starting from the baseline and incrementally adding features
(Table~\ref{tab:levers}). These runs reduce each bug straight from its window,
without the shared crash-grouping front-end of Table~\ref{tab:headlines}, so the
baseline here reads 0.771 rather than the full-pipeline 0.759, and these eight-seed
figures sit below the thirty-seed isolated rates of Section~\ref{ssec:eval-real}
(0.867 baseline, 0.967 full RDD). The results are
clear, even when they do not always favour RDD.

Adding the SPRT oracle and its validation gate reduces false credit from 0.188 to
zero, a result that holds as additional features are introduced. % levers.json oracle_fc_invariance
This demonstrates the oracle's primary strength. Its impact on genuine recovery is
less pronounced: for real bugs, recovery decreases slightly (from 0.771 to
0.729) because the oracle is paired with the baseline's exact crash matcher. For
suppressors, recovery drops to zero, as plain bisection still fails with a masked
seed. Thus, the oracle primarily enhances safety rather than recovery. Recovery
improves with subsequent steps. Replacing the exact matcher with the two-tier
L2$\rightarrow$L3 identity increases genuine recovery on real bugs from 0.729 to
0.938, as it can identify the same crash across different
dumps that exact matching would miss. % levers.json identity_genuine_real 0.2083
The robust minimiser uniquely benefits suppressors, raising recovery from zero to
1.000 by finding a suppressor-free seed where plain \texttt{ddmin} fails. % levers.json minimiser_genuine_suppressor 1.0
Each step maintains zero false credit: the oracle ensures safety, while the
identity layer and minimiser enhance recovery.

\subsection{Off the shelf, end to end}\label{ssec:eval-live}

The reproducible results above also hold live. On the real Zephyr binary with a
live open-weight model, over eight seeds of forty traces, RDD reaches 0.872 genuine
recovery (range $[0.825, 0.925]$), zero false credit, and 0.931 crash-deduplication
accuracy, matching the fixed 0.863.
% scenario_multiseed.json Cost-gating works: the deterministic L2 settles most comparisons, the open-weight model only a few dozen per seed.

\subsection{Threats to validity}\label{ssec:eval-threats}

Three risks qualify these results.
First, internal: both the SPRT oracle and the transition from enumeration to
\texttt{ddmin} are introduced simultaneously between the baseline and the first
step, making it difficult to isolate the oracle's effect on genuine recovery. To
address this, we run both tools on the same channel and attribute to the oracle
only the clearly measured false-credit result. Second, external: the evaluation
uses a single simulated stack, one fault type in the synthetic data, and a modelled
channel, so the findings may not generalise to other stacks or real radio links.
For this reason, we recommend on-hardware and cross-target experiments. Third,
construct: ``genuine'' is defined by the scorer's channel-off ground truth,
treating reproduction as a deterministic crash. Bugs that appear only with channel
noise are scored as non-reproductions, which means we trade recall for the
zero-false-credit guarantee. This is a deliberate and acknowledged bias.

\subsection{Proposed experiments}\label{ssec:eval-next}

These results inform a research plan, with initial steps that do not require
hardware. The primary objective is to validate the channel model under real
conditions by replaying recovered PoCs on actual radios over a software-defined link
to determine if the modelled burst process aligns with observed behaviour. The next
step is to repeat the study on other Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and cellular stacks using
independent public fuzzing logs to assess whether a single calibrated identity
threshold is sufficient for all stacks or requires adjustment for each. Expanding
testing to include more fault types would extend the identity layer beyond the
single crash mechanism used in the synthetic data, making it more representative of
real devices. Another objective is to enhance the judge: replacing the single
open-weight model with a calibrated panel, evaluated against human agreement
(Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohenkappa1960}), may help reduce the over-merging observed
here. The unavoidable limitation, the indifference zone, suggests using an adaptive
trial budget that increases reads only in the noisiest cases, though this offers
limited improvement.
