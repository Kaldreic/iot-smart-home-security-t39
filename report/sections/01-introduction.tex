\section{Introduction}

Modern homes depend on wireless devices such as smart speakers, video doorbells
and thermostats that connect over Bluetooth, Wi-Fi or a cellular network. The
software behind these radios is large, complex and usually closed, so
vulnerabilities are to be expected, and because the devices receive signals over
the air, anyone in range can try to exploit one without a password or prior
access. Many such radios sit inside trusted homes and are rarely updated, so a
single flaw can become a persistent way in.

The standard way to surface such flaws is \emph{fuzzing}: sending a target many
malformed or out-of-order packets and watching for anything that crashes it. The
\textsc{SweynTooth} campaign found eleven new Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) vulnerabilities
in chips from seven vendors and \textsc{BrakTooth} eighteen across thirteen fuzzed Bluetooth
Classic devices from eleven vendors~\cite{sweyntooth2020,braktooth2022}. Detecting a crash, though, does not
give a developer enough to fix it. What a developer needs is a
\emph{proof of concept} (PoC): the minimal set of packets that reliably triggers
the fault. Distilling that from a campaign that sent tens of thousands of packets
and recorded a terse memory dump is the slow part, and
\textsc{AirBugCatcher}~\cite{airbugcatcher2024} is the most recent tool we know
of that automates it. Where it is forced to compromise is the way into our
proposal.

The difficulty is the medium. Re-sending the same packets does not always give
the same result, because the device keeps hidden state and the channel adds
noise. Every reproduction tool depends on an \emph{oracle}~\cite{oracleproblem2015},
the component that decides whether a sequence triggered the bug, and here the
oracle answers inconsistently. Delta debugging~\cite{ddmin2002} is an established
technique for isolating failure-inducing inputs, but its minimality proof treats
the test as a deterministic function of the input, and delta debugging assumes
that adding packets never removes a crash. When either fails it can discard the
critical packet and converge on
an incorrect or empty result. \textsc{AirBugCatcher} avoids the algorithm: it
enumerates small combinations of suspect packets and runs each once, which keeps
the search cheap but forgoes minimality and accepts the first crash that carries the expected identifier.

We introduce \emph{Robust Delta-Debugging} (RDD), which approximates the stable
verdict delta debugging assumes. A truncated Sequential Probability Ratio Test
(SPRT) repeats the noisy ``did it crash?'' question and folds the answers into one
decision with a bounded false-positive rate. Because that decision is only as good
as the tool's ability to tell whether two crash dumps come from the same bug, a
two-tier identity oracle pairs a rule-based matcher with a single open-weight
language model for the cases the matcher cannot settle. A suppressor-robust driver
keeps the search alive where a packet conceals the crash, and nothing is credited
until an independent re-confirmation reproduces it under the same bound.

The report makes four contributions. It identifies the oracle, rather than the
search strategy, as the binding constraint in wireless reproduction under noise of
the kind the simulator assumes. It
introduces RDD, a pipeline whose false-credit rate is bounded by a tunable
$\alpha$ under stated preconditions. It evaluates a prototype against a
re-implementation of \textsc{AirBugCatcher}'s reproduction strategy on six real
Zephyr controller bugs under a simulated noisy channel, where RDD recovers 0.848
of reproductions with no false credit in 336 campaigns against 0.759 and 0.137,
at five times the device reads, and on a 75,000-campaign synthetic sweep, where
it recovers 0.824 against 0.266; it then shows that the advantage is a property
of the simulator's two noise assumptions, since with report variation and
phantom crashes switched off the baseline recovers 0.978 with no false credit at
a fifth of the cost. % headtohead.json, resilience.json, benchmarks.run sensitivity
Finally it turns those dependencies and the method's structural limits into a
research agenda. The targets are public, and the benchmark code, the reference results and the
